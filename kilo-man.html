Meet Kilo Man: 10 centimeters tall, about a kilogram, and very determined. He's living proof that it doesn't matter how small you are.

Okay, full honesty: Kilo Man doesn't have a body yet. Right now he's borrowing one. The figure you see in every screenshot is Unreal Engine's free practice mannequin, shrunk down to 10 cm and doing a very brave impression of our hero. Kilo Man will get his own custom model and his own animations. Until then, the mannequin keeps his spot warm.

Kilo Man today

  • His size feels right. He moves like a tiny hero, and blades of grass tower over him like trees. {% include status.html s="Working" %}
  • His own camera. It follows close behind him with a wide view, so the garden feels enormous. It was tuned by feel over a few rounds of play-testing. {% include status.html s="Working" %}
  • Running looks right for his size. His run animation is sped up to match his tiny steps. {% include status.html s="Working" %}
  • He can get hurt. He has 3 HP, with a squish and a small "oof" on every hit. {% include status.html s="Working" %}
  • His look. Still borrowed. The mannequin is a stand-in and isn't the real Kilo Man. His own custom model and animations are coming. {% include status.html s="Planned" %}

How a new character gets made

Here's the path a game character takes from an idea to something you can play, step by step. The labels show where Kilo Man is on each one. New words? Try the glossary.

  1. The idea {% include status.html s="Working" %}

    Who is he? A tiny, brave hero: 10 cm, 1 kg, heading for a pickle jar the size of a mountain. Size and personality come first, because everything else depends on them.

  2. Concept sketches {% include status.html s="Planned" %}

    Drawings of him from the front, side and back, plus a few poses and expressions. This is where his look gets decided before anyone builds anything.

  3. The 3D model {% include status.html s="Planned" %}

    The sketches get turned into a 3D shape, a bit like digital clay. Then a lighter, tidier version is made that a game can draw quickly, which matters for modest PCs.

  4. Colors and textures {% include status.html s="Planned" %}

    Paint gets wrapped around the model, the way a label wraps around a jar.

  5. The skeleton ("rig") {% include status.html s="Planned" %}

    An invisible skeleton goes inside the model so it can bend at the knees, elbows and neck. If his skeleton matches the stand-in's, he can reuse the moves the game already has.

  6. Animation {% include status.html s="Planned" %}

    Run, jump, land, climb, get bonked. For now he's using the mannequin's moves, sped up for his tiny legs. Kilo Man will get his own custom animations, ideally with a bit more personality than a crash-test dummy.

  7. Into the game {% include status.html s="Working" %}

    The hard parts of being tiny are already built: his size, weight, camera and movement. When his real model is ready, it slips into that setup like a brand-new costume, and the mannequin can finally go home.

  8. Play-test and polish {% include status.html s="In progress" %}

    Run, fall, retry, tweak, repeat. Nothing counts as working until it's been played, and this step never really ends.
