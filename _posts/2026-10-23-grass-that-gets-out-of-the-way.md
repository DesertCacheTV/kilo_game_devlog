**TL;DR:** The flowers stay planted now, even when we stretch a patch up big. And Kilo Man has grass: our own home-made tufts, taller than he is, that lean out of his way as he runs through.
{: .tldr}

Last time the flower patch floated or sank when we stretched it. Tonight we fixed that, and then we tried to grow grass. The grass fought back a little.

## Flowers that stay planted {% include status.html s="Fixed" %}

The flower patch used to look for the ground in a very small area. Make the patch bigger, or move it up or down, and it lost the ground. The flowers ended up floating, or buried.

Now each flower picks its spot once and looks for the ground right there. You can scale a patch up big and the flowers stay put. I played it, and it looks very good.

{% include figure.html src="/assets/img/kilo-flowers-stay-planted.jpg" alt="Big flowers beside the trail in play, standing on the ground after the fix." caption="Flowers, staying where they belong." width="1251" height="1129" %}

## Our own grass {% include status.html s="Working" %}

Our first try used an old single blade of grass. It looked awful. (More on that below.)

So we made our own grass tuft from scratch. Each tuft is six blades fanned out from one root. They taper to a point and curve a little, like real grass. The color runs from dark green at the bottom to a warm, sunny yellow-green at the tips.

Next to a 10 cm hero, one tuft is taller than he is. I love how it looks.

{% include figure.html src="/assets/img/kilo-grass-tufts-new.jpg" alt="The new grass tufts in play: curved blades, dark green at the roots and yellow-green at the tips." caption="Home-made grass, first look." width="1115" height="770" %}

## Grass that leans out of the way {% include status.html s="In progress" %}

There was one problem. The grass grew straight up through the trail, so Kilo Man ran right through it like a ghost.

Now the grass leans away from him as he passes, and springs back behind him. The bigger the grass patch, the wider it parts, so giant grass still makes room for a tiny hero.

Grass patches also got new shapes. They don't have to be big circles anymore. They can be half circles, rings with an empty middle, or long strips for trail edges and backgrounds. Far-away grass can also fade out, so the game stays light on small laptops.

My first run through it: fantastic. Now I need to dial it all in, one piece at a time.

## Blooper of the day

The first grass looked so bad that, in the dark, it was genuinely spooky. We wrote "spooky night grass" on the idea list.

Also: our very first home-made tuft showed up in the game lying flat on its side, like it needed a nap.
